# Anti-slop audit 001

Scope: the published home page, About page source, shared navigation/footer, and responsive stylesheet.

No portfolio design files were changed during this audit.

## Findings

1. **HIGH — No explicit design direction (R-37).** The site has a palette and type choices, but no `DESIGN.md`, written identity, or ENERGY/RHYTHM/MOTION values, so there is no source of truth for deciding whether a visual choice belongs to Marsha's portfolio.

2. **HIGH — Em dashes appear in visible copy (R-02).** The two experience date ranges use `—`; the rule requires commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or another natural construction instead.

3. **HIGH — Footer icon links have no accessible names (R-32).** In the rendered accessibility tree, the email, LinkedIn, and GitHub links are announced only as unnamed links, so keyboard and screen-reader users cannot tell what each control does.

4. **HIGH — Navigation contrast is not reliable across header states (R-25).** The fixed navigation uses white text while the captured full-page state gives it a light translucent background, leaving insufficient visual separation and no documented contrast verification for the changing backdrop.

5. **MEDIUM — Typography lacks a written identity reason (R-06).** Lora, Open Sans, and the system sans stack are mixed across headings, labels, navigation, and body copy, but their roles are not defined; this contributes to the font inconsistency already noticed during earlier revisions.

6. **MEDIUM — The technical grid background is decorative rather than specific (R-07).** The masthead grid suggests a generic engineering interface but is not connected to Marsha's research, data work, or personal identity by a stated purpose.

7. **MEDIUM — Research is flattened into identical cards (R-14).** Both research items use the same size, padding, numbering, and structure even though the MPhil work is more recent and more relevant to the intended AI/data-engineering positioning.

8. **MEDIUM — Direction arrows are repeated as a default link treatment (R-08).** “View repository” and “More about me” both end in arrows without a documented navigation purpose; the pattern reads as decoration rather than information.

9. **LOW — Section composition is too uniform (R-05).** Most sections repeat an uppercase eyebrow, large serif heading, and bordered or ruled content underneath, so research, projects, and experience have similar visual rhythm despite representing different kinds of evidence.

10. **LOW — The portfolio still reads as a customized theme rather than a personal system (R-20).** The inherited masthead, Bootstrap buttons, numbered cards, circular social icons, and copyright footer could be reused for another person with only the text changed.

11. **LOW — Major visual decisions are undocumented (R-31).** The teal palette, full-width About band, square buttons, CTA ring, generous spacing, and project-card hover lift have no one-line purpose statements, making future revisions prone to inconsistency.

## Suggested design read for the fix phase

This is not a final direction and must be approved before implementation:

Portfolio for AI and data-engineering recruiters and technical collaborators, grounded in an editorial research-document visual language, with ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1.

The intent would be to make research and working systems the strongest evidence, keep the reading experience calm, and vary section structure enough to distinguish research, applied projects, and production experience.
